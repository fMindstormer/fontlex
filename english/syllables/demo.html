Fontlex English Syllables

A plain text box, in a font that colors each word's syllables in turn, blue and red, so a child learning to read sees where to break a long word: lit-tle, ti-ger, but-ter-flies. Edit the text, type your own, or use the font in a word processor to make worksheets.

How it decides

  • Words split as dictionaries split them: a vowel that ends a syllable is long (ti-ger), a short one keeps the consonant after it (rab-bit), and a consonant before -le starts the last syllable (ta-ble).
  • The splits come from 47,741 hyphenation patterns, trained the way TeX's are, on the Moby Hyphenation List (about 160,000 words) with the frequent words it lacks added: one-syllable words, unsplit, and inflected forms like start-ed and play-ing. The text engine runs them as you type, so the font also guesses at words it was never shown.
  • Every word starts blue. Everything happens inside the font; no script on this page does the coloring.

How often it's right

Counted against the word list, so this measures how faithfully the font follows a dictionary, not a teacher's judgment:

WordsSplit exactly as the list does
Frequent words: those of the 30,000 most frequent that the list has27,444 of 27,536 (99.7%)
Frequent words held out of training2,505 of 2,790 (89.8%)
All words held out of training14,126 of 16,833 (83.9%)

So a common word comes out as the dictionary splits it, and a word the list lacks is right about five times in six. Teachers haven't reviewed it yet.

Limits

  • Dictionary hyphenation isn't always how a word is taught or said: the list has lat-er where phonics teaches la-ter, and eve-ry where a reader says ev-ry.
  • Words with an apostrophe (don't) are split at it.
  • It is slower to lay out than a plain font, about 22 ms for a 100-word paragraph: fine for typing, slow for a long document.
  • Needs an app that renders color fonts with OpenType features: current Chrome, Firefox and Safari do, and so do Pages and TextEdit on a Mac.

Word list: the Moby Hyphenation List by Grady Ward (public domain). Pronunciations used to check syllable counts: CMUdict. Base font: Noto Sans (SIL Open Font License). How it's made and measured: english/syllables/README.md.

Fontlex French

A font that hints at how French words are pronounced, for learners. The text box below holds plain text, in French; the font draws the hints, in any app that renders color fonts. Copy the text and you get the letters you typed.

Silent letters fade: the t of petit, the ent of ils parlent. The letters of a nasal vowel are violet: an, on, ain, un, so bon and bonne look different. A schwa that speakers often drop (samedi, petit) is blue. A liaison, a final consonant said before the next word's vowel, is bold and green, with a tie under the space: les amis, nous avons, vont-ils. French has no word stress to mark. The switches above the text turn one kind of hint off; in other apps they are the font's stylistic sets (ss01 silent letters, ss02 schwas, ss04 nasal vowels, ss05 liaisons).

Where the hints come from

The pronunciations are Lexique 3.83's. Each word's spelling is aligned with its sounds, letter groups such as eau, ou, an, gn and ill taken as one spelling of one sound, so a letter that spells no sound is silent. The font lists the 30,000 most frequent words that Lexique has, about 94% of running French text, and 129 compounds such as peut-être; any other word is drawn plain. Liaison is drawn only where it is obligatory: after 42 determiners, pronouns, short prepositions and numbers (les, nous, dans, deux), and in inversion; not before an h aspiré (les héros) or onze. A word whose verb reading differs (le président, ils président) takes its reading from the word before it.

How a font does it

OpenType passes. Right to left, each word's last letter gets a variant that says so; left to right, its first letter. Then cue words and words that block liaison are marked; verb/noun pairs read their cue; liaison triggers look one space ahead for a vowel or an h muet. Last, one rule per listed word, matching from a first letter to a last one with case ignored, applies a hint to each marked letter.
